A holiday home with a fully equipped kitchen in Saigon

A fridge of about 540 litres, stove, oven, microwave, kettle and rice cooker, plus dishes, cookware and a dining table for everyone. Bring fresh herbs from the market and cook what you like, when you like.

A closer look at the kitchen

The kitchen and dining area form one bright, open space. The wall behind the worktop is covered in bold black-and-white patterned tiles. On the counter, a black kettle, a knife block, a stainless-steel utensil bin and a rack of cooking utensils stand ready. Behind the cabinet doors are pots, pans and rows of glasses, and the tall dark fridge of about 540 litres stands beside the counter.

The dining table has a white marble-look top on a black cross-shaped base. Upholstered chairs in light sage and cream stand around it on a geometric rug. Glass doors with dark grey curtains let in daylight, and a large potted plant stands by the wall. White walls with grey vertical panelling and matte-black doors carry the house colours into the kitchen. A front-loading washing machine stands in the corner.

A real kitchen, not a kitchenette

Many city apartments give you a kettle and a microwave and call it a kitchen. At ASCON LIVING you get the real thing: a stove, an oven, a microwave, a rice cooker, a kettle and cupboards with plates, bowls and other dishes. Everything you need to cook a proper meal is already there, so you only need to bring the ingredients.

The patterned tiles and the bright counter make it a kitchen you actually want to spend time in, whether you are preparing a quick breakfast or a slow dinner for a crowd.

The large fridge

The side-by-side fridge holds approx. 540 litres. That is enough for a group's groceries, a week of family breakfasts, fruit from the market, and the drinks and marinated meat for tonight's rooftop BBQ, all at once. No more playing fridge Tetris with someone else's leftovers.

Cook like a local

Some of the best meals in Saigon start at the market.

Market to table

Bring back fresh herbs, limes, chillies, rice noodles and whatever looked best at the stall, and cook it your way. The rice cooker makes perfect rice for everyone, and the stove is ready for a stir-fry or a simple soup. Cooking with local ingredients is one of the most enjoyable ways to get to know Vietnamese food culture.

For food lovers and couples

Try your hand at a homemade phở broth on a lazy day, or roll fresh spring rolls together at the counter. Cooking side by side, with music on and a cold drink, is a date night in itself.

Family meals made easy

Travelling with children often means the question "what are we going to eat?" comes up five times a day. A full kitchen answers it.

Breakfast exactly how the kids like it

Toast, eggs, cereal, fruit, warm milk: prepare the breakfast your children know from home and start the day without a hungry wait at a restaurant. Snacks, yoghurt and juice keep fresh in the large fridge.

Familiar food after new flavours

After a week of exciting new dishes, everyone sometimes needs pasta. Cook a family favourite in the oven or on the stove, and let the adventurous eaters bring back street food for themselves.

Baby food and special diets

Warm a bottle in the microwave, prepare purées or cook for allergies and dietary preferences without having to explain them in a foreign language.

Around the dining table

The dining table by the kitchen windows is where the moments of a trip come together.

Slow breakfasts and long dinners

Sit down for a relaxed breakfast before a big day out, or gather for a shared dinner where everyone tells the story of their day. In a private house, nobody is waiting for your table.

For groups: cook together, eat together

Split the shopping, assign the jobs and turn dinner into a team project. One group cooks, the next one washes up. Afterwards the table becomes the place for a round of cards, a board game or the planning of tomorrow's route.

For long stays and remote workers

Staying a week or longer? Having your own kitchen makes a real difference to both comfort and budget. Cook a simple lunch between calls instead of going out in the midday heat.

From the kitchen to the rooftop

On warm evenings, carry the food upstairs and fire up the BBQ or smoker on the rooftop terrace, with the river and the District 1 skyline as the backdrop. And on the nights when nobody wants to cook, restaurants and street food are close by.

Frequently asked questions

Is the kitchen fully equipped?

Yes. There is a stove, an oven, a microwave, a rice cooker, a kettle, a large fridge of approx. 540 litres and dishes for the whole group.

Can we cook our own meals during the stay?

Of course. The kitchen is part of the house you book, and only your group uses it.

Is there space for everyone to eat together?

Yes, the dining table by the kitchen windows brings the group together, and on warm evenings the rooftop terrace is a second dining room.
