The living room of your whole house rental in Ho Chi Minh City

About 40 m², a large L-shaped sofa, a 75-inch 4K TV and nobody else around. This is where your day in Saigon begins, and where everyone gathers again when the city lights come on.

What's in this room

  • About 40 m² of living space
  • Large white L-shaped sofa with olive-green and white cushions
  • 75-inch 4K smart TV with Netflix
  • PlayStation with PlayStation Plus
  • Black glass coffee table
  • Floor-to-ceiling glass doors with dark grey curtains
  • Air conditioning and a standing fan
  • Guest WC in the living area
  • Colour concept: grey, white and greige with matte-black accents

A closer look at the living room

The living room is long, bright and open. One side is almost all glass: floor-to-ceiling glass doors with dark grey curtains open onto the green outside and fill the room with daylight. The large white L-shaped sofa, scattered with olive-green and white cushions, faces the 75-inch 4K smart TV. The TV hangs on a white wall between two tall panels of matte-black vertical slats, above a low grey-and-black console.

A black glass coffee table stands on a light rug in the middle of the room. Potted plants with large leaves flank the TV, and a colourful framed painting and a round wall clock hang on the walls. Warm LED cove lighting runs around the ceiling for the evenings. The floor is grey marble-look tile. A wall-mounted air conditioner and a standing fan keep the room comfortable, and the guest WC is just off the living area.

A living room big enough for everyone

In a hotel, "together" usually means squeezing onto the end of a bed or meeting in a noisy lobby. At ASCON LIVING the living room is yours alone, and at about 40 m² it is big enough for the whole family or group to sit together without anyone perched on an armrest. The large L-shaped sofa is the centre of the room: deep enough to stretch out after a long day of sightseeing, long enough for everyone to find a place during movie night.

Plants and the glass doors keep the space light and relaxed rather than formal. It is a room meant to be lived in, not just looked at.

Room to spread out

Bags from the market on the floor, a laptop on one end of the sofa, kids building a blanket fort on the other, someone reading in the corner. Nobody is in anyone's way, and nobody has to ask a front desk for a bigger room.

A guest WC right where you need it

The guest WC in the living area is one of those small details you appreciate every day. Children, visitors and anyone who just wants to wash their hands before dinner can use it without heading upstairs to a bedroom bathroom.

Movie nights and match days on the 75-inch 4K TV

The 75-inch 4K TV turns the living room into the natural meeting point of the house. Netflix is ready to stream, so you can pick up the series you started at home or discover something new together.

For families: cartoon mornings and family films

Let the kids start the day with their favourite show while breakfast is being made in the kitchen. In the evening, dim the lights, hand out snacks and enjoy a family film with everyone together on one sofa.

For groups: the big match, together

Whether it is football, a Formula 1 race or a final everyone has been waiting for, the big screen and the big sofa make the living room the best seat in the house. Cold drinks wait in the large kitchen fridge.

For couples: a quiet night in

Not every evening in Saigon has to be a night out. Sometimes the best plan is a film, a bowl of fresh fruit from the market and the whole living room to yourselves.

Slow mornings and lazy afternoons

Ho Chi Minh City runs at full speed. The living room is where you slow down.

Coffee before the city wakes

Make a coffee or tea with the kettle, settle into a corner of the sofa and plan the day in peace. Some guests like to look through the photos from yesterday, others simply enjoy the quiet before the rest of the house gets up.

The midday break from the heat

Saigon afternoons can be hot and humid. Instead of looking for a café to escape the sun, come back home, put your feet up and recharge for the evening. A nap on the sofa is allowed, and in a private house nobody will notice.

When the rain comes

During the rainy season, short and heavy showers are part of daily life. With a living room this comfortable, a sudden downpour is not a problem but a good excuse for a board game, a series or a long conversation.

A living room that works as an office

Travelling for work, or staying longer and working remotely? The living room adapts.

For remote workers: fast mesh WiFi

WiFi of up to 1 Gbit/s with mesh on every floor means a stable connection for video calls, uploads and streaming at the same time. Answer emails on the sofa in the morning, join a call in the afternoon, and close the laptop when the others come back from exploring.

For business groups: a meeting room with a sofa

Teams on a business trip can use the living room for a morning briefing or an evening review of the day, then switch to the big TV for a presentation or simply for a match after work. It is more relaxed than a hotel conference room, and nobody books it out from under you.
